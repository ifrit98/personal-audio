# Felix Zulauf: One More Rally Left Before Major Market Correction Next Year

- **Guest:** Felix Zulauf (founder/CEO, Zulauf Consulting)
- **Host / show:** Adam Taggart, Thoughtful Money
- **Published:** 2026-11-18
- **Length:** 1h09m
- **Source:** whisper.cpp transcript, no timestamps or speakers. "Next year" = 2027.

## Thesis

Zulauf says the current tech/AI correction is a normal medium-term pullback that is close to bottoming, with the S&P 500 in the very low 7,000s by late 2026 or January. He expects one more rally to new highs above 8,000 in Q1 or early Q2 2027. He calls this the last leg of the cycle that began in 2022. After that, a liquidity squeeze plus extreme index concentration should produce a panic decline in 2027 that shocks the world, with S&P down to about 5,000 and Nasdaq 100 down 30-50%. That decline triggers a huge fiscal and monetary stimulus ("mother of all stimulation"). He sees the S&P doubling to about 10,000 over 2028-29, ending a multi-generational cycle. After that comes a rising-yield, systemic and geopolitical crisis, with a 1968-74-style bear market in the 2030s. He still calls the AI investment boom intact but moderating, with the earnings problem in 2028.

## Main arguments

### Near-term equities and AI
- He says he turned bullish in April 2025 after the S&P fell below 5,000, and projected 7,500 then, which many thought was crazy.
- The correction he expected since summer is "developing according to plan". Targets: S&P 6,900-7,200 (about 7,100 ballpark); Nasdaq 100 about 26,000 or slightly below.
- Sentiment has moved from complacency to fear and liquidation. He thinks aggressive money is largely out of AI and tech, which he sees as healthy.
- He thinks a medium-term bottom comes by late 2026, possibly extending into January, "in the very low 7,000s" on the S&P.
- Nvidia: his target of about 150 has been reached. He wouldn't be surprised by +50% to +100% over about six months, though he says he's not a bottom-up guy.
- He says the economy has been overheating from the AI investment boom, which is the biggest investment boom in US history, bigger than railroads, internet and computers.
- Credit demand is very strong while credit supply is scarcer. This pushed yields up, which he says is the bond market forcing a slowdown. He adds that the Middle East war and higher oil also slowed the economy.
- Hyperscaler free cash flow "declined like a stone"; they fund buildout with debt and equity issuance, and shareholders objected.
- 2027: orders already signed, so tech earnings are OK, but investment growth slows at the margin.
- 2028 is the earnings problem. New semiconductor capacity from big players arrives from 2028, forcing chip prices, margins and estimates down.
- He calls current tech profit margins "70% or something" unsustainable (garbled in transcript).
- He says AI is like earlier tech booms. Overbuilding is followed by lower service prices and weak ROE for early players, and some fade or fail. The economy then grows into the technology, with productivity trending at about 1% long term.

### The 2027 top and panic
- The Q1/Q2 2027 high is the last rally. The market's top is concentrated: a few stocks are 40% of the S&P and tech is over 50%.
- Liquidity squeeze means a 20% correction can become 30%, "maybe even 50%" for the Nasdaq 100.
- He expects a "panic decline" similar to 1998, with possible "black swans". The decline starts mid-2027 from the Q2 high.
- Authorities respond once the market is down about 20%, fearing system fragility. Fiscal moves from "high deficits to higher deficits" and easier money to "very easy".
- Low for the S&P is late 2027 / early 2028, perhaps 5,000. This is followed by a 1-2 year run to about 10,000.
- Positioning advice (he declines to recommend anything specific yet): from the first months of 2027, prefer the most defensive equities, not cyclicals. Re-buy after the panic once stimulus appears, "probably a year from now, a good year from now". Tech may well lead the next run.

### Rates, inflation, bonds, dollar
- Secular trend is rising rates and inflation, because of deglobalization, protectionism, sanctions, wars and deficits. The 40-year decline in rates is over.
- He called for 10-year yields of 5.25-5.50% "and we are there". He told subscribers 10-year yields are topping.
- Over the next 12 months he expects yields to fall 150-200bp. That would bring a 10-year "three handle", which he confirmed with "Yes, absolutely". He calls it the first cyclical correction within a rising trend.
- Inflation is stuck around 3% as the new level, easing from about 4%, with oil's reversal as a help. Bonds become the flight-to-safety destination when stocks fall.
- He says stocks and yields normally rise and fall together within a cycle, contrary to mainstream media.
- Trade: build duration now. Better still for a USD investor are 10-year Bunds and JGBs, which add currency gains.
- Dollar: the Dollar Index has been in an uptrend channel since 2008. He expects a downside break in 2027 and sharp selling against Asian and European currencies.
- Reason: surplus nations no longer buy Treasuries; they put surpluses into US tech stocks via sovereign wealth funds, at record levels. A US equity decline would reverse these flows.
- After late 2027, the next up-cycle in yields breaks "decisively" above 5-5.5% and could reach about 8%, worldwide. The system is "more leveraged than ever" and cannot absorb it, and it will be the market, not policy, that ends it.
- He says several governments will default in the first half of the 2030s.

### Gold, silver, commodities, Bitcoin
- He says he warned subscribers about a year ago that gold's rise would become climactic. Gold peaked in January 2026 (transcript garbled "'55, '95"), fell to the low-to-mid 4,000s, bounced to about 5,400, then he predicted a "frustrating decline" to 3,800-4,000.
- He says gold is now in buying territory, below 4,000, with 3,800 likely and best to buy near 3,800. He says he owns a lot of gold but was bearish all year.
- He expects gold +50% in 2027, to about 6,000.
- Gold may be around 10,000 (his words "probably at 10,000 too") when the S&P hits 10,000. In the early part of the later decline gold keeps rising. In a systemic liquidity squeeze (late 2020s / early 2030s) gold falls too, and "the name of the game is to lose the least".
- Gold is the preferred collateral, as central banks show, and is now the largest central-bank reserve asset, larger than US Treasuries. He also includes gold miners.
- Silver: buy below $50, with a lower target of $44-46. He expects it may outperform gold on the upside, but is more volatile. It is in its seventh year of production deficit, and a Chinese solar manufacturer began switching from silver to copper at the highs. At sub-$50 he thinks that switching stops, but calls this a long-term negative overhang.
- Commodities broadly: topping out, in a bear market for the next 12 months, then a "fantastic place to be" after the stimulus.
- Oil: swings with news between about $60 and $110 for the next few years. He sees a downtrend toward $60 now. Hormuz is about 70-80% open. In a serious war (from 2028 onwards, "late 20s") the Strait closes and oil goes to $200.
- Bitcoin: a trading vehicle rather than an investment, since governments could declare it illegal. He called for a correction below $70,000 (done). Next he expects a medium-term bottom, then about $100,000 in H1 2027, then $40,000 if equities fall from a Q2 high.

### Geopolitics and politics
- He says the world is "multi-polar disorder", and that the West has lost dominance for the first time in 500 years.
- He says the US provoked the Ukraine war through NATO expansion, that Ukraine is a proxy against Russia, and that Russia will win. He gives a four-minute Tomahawk flight time to Moscow as the reason Russia feels threatened.
- He says attacking Iran was unwise. Iran is number two or three in missile technology after Russia, can destroy Gulf oil infrastructure, and the US cannot win militarily. He says the US is short on ammunition, diesel and jet fuel, so he expects only tit-for-tat strikes for now and renewed war in the late 2020s.
- He says Taiwan would not be a problem if the US did not provoke China. He supports the "big three" (US, China, Russia) talking, and expects spheres of influence.
- On political figures: he criticized Trump's handling of Canada (Carney in place), said the Venezuela action was about keeping resources in the Western Hemisphere, and said the Brazil election "went the wrong way". He says presidents don't really call the shots; unnamed "deep state" players do.
- He says China's growth numbers are faked, and that it over-invests and exports excess capacity, which drives Western protectionism and tariffs.
- He says Hong Kong has overtaken Switzerland as the top offshore financial center.
- He expects a major political crisis in the West ("democracies are in a crisis"), capital controls, possible currency reform and asset confiscation. His advice is to stay in a jurisdiction you know.

### Personal and advice
- He prioritizes family and friends over the digital world, and recalls 1968-74 as the true bear market, with the 1973-74 trough "brutal" (NYSE volume very low, New York City near bankruptcy). He expects something like that in the 2030s.
- He warns retirees not to buy what they don't understand, and says Wall Street under-emphasizes risk.
- Follows his work by going to his website; Zulauf Consulting sends reports about every two weeks.

- Tension: he says commodities are in a bear market for 12 months, yet projects gold +50% and silver strength in 2027.
