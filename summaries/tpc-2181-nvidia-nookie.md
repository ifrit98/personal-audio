- **Speakers:** host "Tommy" (mostly silent, runs the show); Matt Dines; Tom Luongo; Vince Lanci. The transcript has no speaker labels. Where attribution is uncertain it is marked "a speaker". One speaker (probably Vince) leaves around the 1h45m mark.

## Thesis

The speakers read the sacking of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and his reinstatement "Sunday night" as a power fight, not a scandal over Huang's relationship with a financier. Matt follows the finance plumbing (Hong Kong, Taiwan, Singapore and Malaysia GPU financing, banks, a data-center landlord, CME compute futures). A second speaker (probably Vince, who comes at it from an ideological/business angle) frames it as "little tech" (Anthropic, OpenAI), who want a government-protected moat and nationalized AI, against open-weight, free-market AI, which Huang supports because he sells chips. Both link it to a US dollar squeeze: Fed hiking, tight money markets, AI credit (Oracle) under strain, and a shift to a stablecoin/GENIUS-Act "new dollar". The practical conclusion is two to three more months of tight dollar markets, 50-75bp of hikes by March, and weak gold in the near term.

## Main arguments

### Who went after Huang, and why
- **Timeline (Matt):** the "Jensen cheating" story was planted about a year earlier. Culper Research published in about May. A New York Times piece of about 29 Oct (five or six journalists, including Maggie Haberman and Jonathan Swan) pushed the "Chinese spy" angle. Huang was reinstated Sunday night. Matt calls this "clearly another manufactured event".
- **Matt on the racket:** three nodes (Taiwan chip manufacturing, Hong Kong financing, Singapore/Malaysia data centers) plus US-domiciled banks. Nvidia is described as the "central bank of AI", at the center of circular and vendor financing and equity swaps.
- **Alice Wong [press reports name her Alice Huang] (Matt):** the woman at the center of the NYT story, apparently "Quang Lei or Alice Wong". She has a finance background (HSBC private wealth in Hong Kong, then "Civic Securities", then portfolio management for a private tech fund). Around 2020 she shifted to "Megaspeed", which Matt calls her piece of business. Matt says she was key to the bank loans that financed the Nvidia chip purchases, and he calls her a deal-maker and revenue producer, not just a sex-scandal figure. He says the press reduces the story to "Alice has sex with Jensen, Jensen sells chips to Megaspeed", which he calls high-school-level.
- **Data-center landlord (Matt):** a "BDC" operator that ran the GPUs on the ground. He says JPMorgan and Citi were lenders to the landlord, not to Megaspeed, in the "120.28 million" range, and asks to double-check. He says an Alibaba subsidiary lent to Megaspeed. He adds that the Hong Kong AI IPOs (MiniMax, "Z dot AI") have been "smoked", and speculates that Chinese open-source labs trained in the Malaysian data centers via remote access.
- **BIS rule (Matt):** after the Huang firing and reinstatement, Commerce's Bureau of Industry and Security (Jeffrey Kessler, under Howard Lutnick) issued a rule. Matt says Nvidia was "embedded" and agreed to more monitoring, and that the rule killed the "we sell to the landlord, not China" workaround. He says Treasury and the NSC forced Commerce's hand.
- **Two-faction model (one speaker, probably Vince; Tom endorses it):** "little tech" (OpenAI and Anthropic) wants the government to restrict others from running GPUs locally. It does this by arguing that AI is dangerous, which pushes regulation or nationalization that protects incumbents. Big tech (Microsoft, Google) is the legacy side. The speaker says Huang is "open-AI oriented" (relayed from "the people I talked to") and wants everyone to have AI, so he was an obstacle. The speaker cites Sam Altman's earlier remark that the government would bail them out.
- **Zero Hedge link:** the same speaker cites a Zero Hedge headline, "follow the money: major Anthropic backer bankrolls podcast peddling Cold War spy scandal / Huang hysteria". Dustin Moskovitz is named as the backer (Matt and the speaker discuss his Anthropic ties). The speaker frames it as "Bill Gates vs Steve Jobs" (closed vs open); Tom calls the framing "absolutely correct".
- **Ideology (Tom, in a long rant):** he ties Anthropic and OpenAI to effective altruists and to "Brussels and London" elites. He says P(doom) and "Terminator" narratives are used to justify control. He says the EU earns more from fining and taxing US firms than from its own output.
- **Compute vs models (Tom):** he relays a point from "Dexter White" that China can run three times the data centers on chips a third as good and get the same compute. He says the chips are not what matters. He argues DeepSeek-style narrow-trained models get about 80% of the result for 20% of the compute, which is what the incumbents' moat is meant to block.
- **Over-build analogy (Tom):** he compares the AI build-out to WorldCom fiber, which produced cheap bandwidth and then "Netflix and on-demand porn". He says many Blackwell chips sit in warehouses because data centers are not built, and next-gen chips will arrive first.
- **Why Huang was reinstated:**
  - Tom says the firing was meant to push Nvidia, and then everything else, into a deflationary collapse before the new Congress sits. He says it also served to "set the table" for the EU to consolidate debt and roll European pensions into a digital euro.
  - Matt says Trump cannot dictate to a board, so "a power faction" (banks, AI firms, infrastructure) put Huang back. He names Elon Musk and Travis Kalanick as vocal supporters.
  - Tom agrees it was banks plus AI plus infrastructure, naming Jamie Dimon, Solomon and Goldman. He frames it as "America's leaders acting like they work for America" and says markets were stabilized.

### Anthropic IPO
- **Tom:** he says Anthropic has a share lock-up and public-benefit-corporation structure, with a board of about seven and little real voting control sold. He says its IPO money is "already spent nine times over", and that it could be the "greatest IPO in history" and still be liquidated "for pennies on the dollar within 18 months". Tom says he discussed this with Dexter White and had not fully read the filing. He compares the voting structure to Facebook's Class C shares ("you bought a meme coin").
- **Liquidity:** Tom says the IPO sucks "$200 billion of M0" out of the credit market (the figure is unclear). Matt agrees that "other markets are being cannibalized to save AI" and that dollar liquidity is being created by liquidating other holdings. Both note other IPOs (OpenAI next, Bloomberg).
- **Tom's scenario:** the US government could treat Anthropic as no longer a private concern and take it into the sovereign wealth fund ("Dario, you work for me now"). He says Anthropic should not assume it gets more Nvidia chips, given its backers' role in the attack on Huang.

### Credit and markets
- **Rally without credit (Matt):** Monday rally: Nvidia +31%, Micron +19%. Credit did not follow. Oracle yields hit a new all-time high, with 2033s above 8%. Matt calls it a "bear market rally" and says credit markets are saying "the capacity isn't there".
- **Chip-dollar (a speaker, probably Vince, citing a Deutsche Bank report):** chips now trade more dollars than oil, so a "chip-dollar" cycle replaces the petrodollar: Asian chipmakers earn dollars and recycle them into US stocks. The speaker links this to stablecoins, banks holding more Treasuries, and getting foreigners to buy US stocks if they won't buy bonds. The same speaker said the report notes the ecosystem is fragile.
- **CME compute futures (Matt):** a CME/Nymex GPU-hour futures contract (with "Silicon Data") was targeted for 5 Oct 2026 and delayed, pending the CFTC. Bloomberg's latest report on it was 20 Nov. Matt speculates that bringing Nvidia under BIS control may be a prerequisite for launching it. He also notes that 6 Oct was the day US Treasury yields topped.
- **SOFR curve (Tom):**
  - June 2027 SOFR dropped from 4.69 (Wed) to 4.55 (Fri), then returned to about 4.63.
  - A "hump" between Q2 and Q3 2027 is more pronounced. He reads it as a short-term liquidity crunch, with one or two more hikes before the curve falls off (cuts in 2H 2027).
  - The 1y-2y Treasury spread collapsed from over 30bp at the election to 10bp as of Sunday.
  - The "kink" is moving shorter in duration, which he reads as nearing the end of the dollar squeeze.
- **Money markets and the Fed (Matt):** the 4-week bill auction closed above the policy corridor. Coupon bonds and money markets flipped into tightness after "Epic Fury" (the Iran operation) and "Hormuz". He says Waller floated 50bp. A move to hike is "classic Bagehot" central banking, and the Fed cannot just QE because it is only about 10% of the money market. He says Oracle looks like the weak link in investment-grade credit and its biggest issuer, but does not expect it to default this cycle. He wants a "scalpel" and says QE is a sledgehammer.
- **Commodity roll-over (Matt):** oil is down 26% since election day and copper has "rolled over". He says this frees dollar capacity, then shows up in credit, and uses the 2022 sequence as a model (oil and copper squeeze, then roll over by June).
- **Tom's endgame:** the US raised rates on purpose to create a credit problem "for everybody else", betting it can handle a higher cost of dollar capital than the rest of the world (10-year money at 7%). Matt: if something breaks, how quickly it is rescued shows whether it is an LTCM or a Bear Stearns.
- **Where to look for breakage (Matt):** he cites 2022-23 (UK pensions, FTX, Silvergate, SVB, Signature, Credit Suisse, First Republic) as a pattern. He flags Bitcoin-treasury companies' preferreds and AI-related credit as areas to watch this cycle.

### Stablecoin dollar and politics
- **Matt on "new dollar":** he compares GENIUS (stablecoin law) to the 1963 Silver Certificate act and the phase-out of silver certificates in favor of Federal Reserve notes, and says bank dollars are being de-supported in favor of stablecoin dollars. He says Congress "burned the boats":
  - His agents classified 60 of 100 Senators as stablecoin-positive.
  - The House vote on GENIUS was 308.
  - A "ROAD Act" provision (title 11 of 12) bars a Fed CBDC until 31 Dec 2030.
  - He describes the Nvidia and "Megaspeed" structure as consistent with owning "distribution nodes" (Binance, OKX), with soft or regulatory nationalization.
  - A speaker raised USDT vs "USAT" (GENIUS-compliant vs offshore) and CNH-type stablecoins.
- **Tom on executive power:** the election result is a "farce"; the president has more power than "kings of old" after the Supreme Court's rulings. He says the "most likely outcome" is that the administration "will lose the midterms", and that the first two years of cases were set up to define executive power before then. He expects Fed governor Lisa Cook to be removed. He expects a re-empowered Elizabeth Warren and Bernie Sanders in a Democratic Congress. He talks about "fortress America" and a "$50 trillion war machine".
- **Gold/silver (Tom):** when the gold/silver ratio peaks it is a monetary event, and when it bottoms it is a supply-chain event. Silver is "an industrial metal", and treating it as monetary is a London-era vestige.

## Claims to fact-check, tensions and unsupported claims

- **Facts to verify:**
  - Huang fired and reinstated on a Sunday night, and the NYT article of 29 Oct.
  - A "Culper Research" report in May.
  - Alice Wong [press reports: Alice Huang], "Megaspeed", the "BDC" landlord, JPMorgan and Citi as lenders ("120.28 million"), and an Alibaba subsidiary loan.
  - The BIS rule and its contents.
  - Nvidia +31% and Micron +19% on Monday.
  - Oracle 2033 bonds above 8%.
  - The CME GPU futures delay (5 Oct) and the Bloomberg report of 20 Nov.
  - The Deutsche Bank "chips overtake oil" claim.
  - Oil down 26% since election day.
  - Senate, House and ROAD Act figures (60 of 100, 308, title 11, 31 Dec 2030).
  - The Zero Hedge headline and the Moskovitz funding claim.
- **Unsupported:**
  - That Anthropic and OpenAI orchestrated the attack on Huang, and that he was removed to crash Nvidia and then markets.
  - The claims about Anthropic's finances ("spent nine times over", "$200 billion of M0", "$18 trillion" and "$17 trillion" bailout figures, "hundred billion" for the IPO).
  - That Trump "did not" call the board, and that Dimon and the banks did.
  - That the whole story is a "failed coup" and an "intelligence operation".
  - Matt's banking-link story rests on his own list of lenders and he says "none of us know exactly what's going on". Tom calls the sex angle "nuts and sluts" and a tell.
- **Tension and disagreement:**
  - Matt says he is not sure the open-source vs closed framing explains the firing ("I don't know that it's all about that"); Tom says the framing is "absolutely correct" and "the fundamental thing".
  - One speaker takes the opposite side: if Huang "did what he did" and gave secrets to China, he "paddled himself up the river" and is open to extortion or "cheese for the mouse". Tom and Matt mostly treat the case as manufactured.
  - Tom says the credit crunch means gold will fall, then says gold is not going to zero. One speaker's "gold going to zero" is a hyperbolic aside.
  - A speaker says he stopped following Huang once he decided the US government was involved ("nationalization").
- **Off-topic and crude content:** much of the second half is political rant (Brussels, Europe, "effective altruists"), jokes, and a closing aside that Dario Amodei "is a eunuch" or had testicular cancer. The speaker says he saw it on X; it is unverified and was labeled unverified on air (a Gemini lookup is cited as saying it circulates on Chinese social media). Do not treat it as a claim from the hosts.
- **Possible mix-up:** "we are going to lose the midterms" conflicts with discussion of an already completed "120th Congress" shift and "260 Democrats". It may refer to 2028 or be garbled.
