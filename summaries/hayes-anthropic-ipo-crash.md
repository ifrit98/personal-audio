# Arthur Hayes: Anthropic IPO Will Trigger The Biggest Crash Ever

- **Guest:** Arthur Hayes (building Flop Labs; says he built perpetual swaps and Ethena)
- **Host / show:** Unnamed host (not identified in transcript), New Era Finance Podcast
- **Published:** 2026-11-17
- **Length:** 56 min (whisper.cpp transcript, no timestamps or speaker labels)
- **Note:** The title is clickbait. In the transcript Hayes says the Anthropic IPO is a *test* of AI sentiment. Anthropic stumbling would trigger a crash, then a bailout he expects to be bullish for crypto.

## Thesis

Hayes says Bitcoin is a 24/7 risk asset that gets sold first in any global risk-off event, so the current slide is forced cash-raising tied to the Nasdaq and AI stocks. He treats the Anthropic IPO as the bellwether. A strong debut means "long everything". A weak one or a pulled IPO means an AI and credit unwind he compares to 1929, 1873 and 1907. He believes (99% confident) that governments cannot allow that deflation and will bail out and print, which he expects to send crypto and gold far higher. His advice is to hold cash, use no leverage, survive the drawdown, then "go long the bailout". The second half of the interview is mostly his new project, Flop Labs, a compute-backed currency for AI agents.

## Main arguments

### Why crypto is falling now
- Crypto trades 24/7, so it is the most liquid asset to sell for margin calls and cash. "You sell what you can, not what you want to sell."
- Bitcoin is beta to the Nasdaq. Nasdaq "really breaking down" in early October; Bitcoin followed.
- "Cosby" (probably CoreWeave) is down about 50% since June/July. He calls it a leading indicator and highly leveraged to the AI story. Bitcoin is not down 50%.
- Bitcoin printed a new 3-4 month low. The host says Hayes sold his positions "a few weeks ago".
- He expects Bitcoin to be less hurt than US tech stocks: "not apocalyptic for Bitcoin".

### The Anthropic IPO as bellwether
- He says the roadshow starts "today" and the IPO should happen in one or two weeks. He asks whether it happens, where it opens, how it prices and how the market reacts.
- Rumors say the valuation is getting a haircut. He cites Wall Street Journal and Financial Times stories on investors reacting negatively to the raw results against the valuation.
- He compares it to SpaceX earlier in 2026. The stock went from about $135 to $200 in the first week and a half ("he who sells first sells best"), which validated AI and brought the bulls back. He says Anthropic is not in that situation: "too many questions", "their business is too shitty".
- He disagrees with the host's SpaceX analogy of liquidity being sucked out beforehand: "I don't think it's a liquidity sucking event for Anthropic." The question is whether the IPO gets out the door at all.
- Hayes says Anthropic is in the "overpaying for compute" trade: hyperscalers and neoclouds hold non-cancelable contracts, and Anthropic owes hundreds of billions to trillions in compute.
- The S-1 reportedly shows payment obligations stepping up materially through the 2030s and 2040s.
- Cash-flow test: can Anthropic raise the money, in debt markets and with an investment-grade rating, to buy the compute month after month? He is betting it cannot. Without the compute, "the whole story breaks apart".
- Unit economics: he says one profitable quarter, then a loss in Q3. He asks whether the company being underwritten is the one that lost money or the one profitable quarter.
- Against the "infinite TAM" bull case (host cites revenue "1 trillion, going to 2 trillion", likely garbled): the question is "cash flow" and how many customers will pay frontier prices.

### AI as commodity
- Chinese models are "good enough" at roughly 10% (later "10%, 1%") of the price of Anthropic and OpenAI. The bet is that customers want the most expensive product.
- Tokens are commodity "oil". Overbuilding means compute prices plummet and agents get used more, but that does not mean Anthropic makes more money.
- Dot-com analogy: Cisco and the fiber builders were not the winners. Google, Facebook and Amazon were, and some did not yet exist in 2001. The AI "Google or Facebook" is yet to be created.

### Bailout and macro mechanism
- Taiwan has already done a "mini bailout". Last Friday the "national team" was announced to buy stocks, and short selling was halted. Hayes says short-sale bans never actually work.
- AI is global, so every government is tethered to it and "they will all bail it out. The question is when."
- US economy: "AI CapEx, that's it." It is financialized and highly levered to the stock market. Without a rising Nasdaq, the debt of neoclouds and hyperscalers gets questioned.
- Sequence he expects: Anthropic bust, Nasdaq falls, Fed cuts and restarts money printing.
- In 2001 the Fed cut and stocks kept falling. Money does not go to indebted AI companies; investors buy staples, utilities and value, pay down debt.
- The liquidity then flows to unloved assets. Crypto is a scarce, fixed-supply asset that "absorbs the liquidity and massively outperforms".
- Gold also rallies. Real-economy data and GDP are "irrelevant" to price action (host adds GDP is fabricated).
- Scale of crash if it breaks: 1929, 1873, 1907 style. AI capex touches North Asian exporters, Southeast Asian and South American data centers, and African mining, so there would be a global deflationary event.
- Austerity tail: Hoover and Mellon. Bitcoin to $10,000 and S&P to 500 is possible. Gold fell less than the S&P in early Depression but still fell. Hayes says the austerity probability is very low: economists "learned the lesson", and politicians who impose it are removed. He has hedged that tail with volatility hedge funds.
- Reference scenario: March 2020. You must survive the "down 50% day" and then the big print follows. Also "S&P at 666 in March 2009".

### Positioning advice
- Now: raise cash, zero or little leverage, stay liquid, be ready to redeploy into large-cap altcoins and Bitcoin when the bailout comes or if Anthropic does well.
- Questions to ask yourself: can you survive the downside, did you use leverage, are your "bridges" (likely "bags") too big?
- Not the time to speculate on narratives. Revisit in 3-6 months once macro improves.
- Hayes's own approach: aim for about 100% return, not 100x. He says he did more than 100% this year in his directional book (realized P&L).
- He holds Bitcoin, Ethereum and coins above $1B market cap with product-market fit and "Lindy". He expects about 2x in a 3-6 month rebound and avoids meme coins.
- Hyperliquid is beta to Bitcoin: "if Bitcoin goes down, Hyperliquid goes down, but if Bitcoin goes up, Hyperliquid goes up way more".
- DeFi will not escape a falling market. Agentic use is "green shoots", and good DeFi prices in the next 3-6 months could be asymmetric.

### Crypto and AI, tokens, VCs
- Public blockchains vs closed systems: closed bank systems have tech debt and zero-days, and AI will break reliance on them. Open source can be audited by "eight billion people or however many agents".
- Government stance on public chains varies. A bank cyberattack could push the argument that open systems would have saved bailout trillions.
- Hayes's critique of token launches: ICO phase (EOS raised about $4B and built nothing), then VC rounds, then high-FDV low-float coins that hit cliffs and die. "Retail is done" with these. Buybacks without revenue mean little (host).
- Hyperliquid as the model: big airdrop, no VC, 97% of fees to token holders; Trump encouraging them to come to the US, CFTC accepting its perps (his claims).
- VCs still have a role (TVL, capital), but the predatory model is ending.

### Flop Labs (his project)
- Goal: "elephant hunting". Previously built perpetual swaps (BitMEX) and Ethena (the dollar stablecoin).
- Thesis: agents need their own payment and settlement currency. It will not be a stablecoin or Bitcoin.
- Agents are like humans. Humans need calories, so they accept fiat that converts to food. Agents need compute, so they will hold a currency directly convertible to compute.
- Mechanism: a network where people contribute GPUs/CPUs/TPUs and do inference ("proof of useful inference"), earning block rewards in "flop". Flop is connected to a decentralized, censorship-resistant spot compute market.
- Price mechanics, like Bitcoin: if flop rises, the nominal flop per gigaflop of compute falls; the compute price does not rise.
- Why not stablecoins: (1) the issuer can freeze an address, "death for an agent" (cites Circle and sanctions); (2) a dollar's value has nothing to do with compute's value; (3) it is human-set interest rates and a bank derivative that agents can't comprehend.
- Same logic for robots and machines. They need fast, programmatic, non-revocable settlement, so a public chain and cryptocurrency, "hopefully flop". Sui's pro-stablecoin agent thesis is the counterposition raised by the host.
- Bootstrap: testnet "in a few weeks"; agents call a faucet for test flop tokens; compute free for 90 days; using it earns an airdrop and stake. Integrations into open-source libraries and harnesses (Hermes), and brokers such as OpenRouter that hide flop from end customers.
- After testnet, compute stays near-free because block rewards subsidize miners, who undercut other providers. This draws demand, speculators buy the token, and a three-party flywheel follows.
- Team is paid every block post-mainnet and loses if it goes to zero. He calls this "the only true AI play where everyone gets in at the same ground level". Anthropic's IPO, by contrast, offers about 30% upside at a "trillion or one and a half" valuation.
- His claim: if trillions of agents each hold some flop, the network becomes "more valuable than Bitcoin". Flop goes to zero if useful inference fails.
- Complaint motivating it: Elon, Sam and Dario "stole" human civilization's data without paying; ordinary people get job losses, data centers in the backyard and inflation.
