# The AI Bubble Is About to Force the Biggest Money Print Yet

- **Guest:** Lawrence (Larry) Lepard, Equity Management Associates (gold, silver, Bitcoin fund)
- **Show / host:** What Bitcoin Did, host Danny
- **Published:** 2026-11-20 (recorded the day of the Anthropic IPO pricing, the day after Nvidia earnings)
- **Length:** 1h17m
- **Source:** whisper.cpp transcript, no timestamps or speaker labels; sponsor reads omitted

## Thesis

Lepard says AI is a real, world-changing technology but also the latest credit bubble, built on cheap money and now being broken by tighter money. He compares it to 2000 and 2008. He sees CoreWeave's slide and its CDS blowout as the first crack, like the 2007 Bear Stearns CDS funds. He expects the AI unwind to hit stocks and bonds, and the Fed to respond with a bigger "big print" (bailouts, yield curve control, stimulus checks). He says US debt is mathematically unpayable, so inflating it away is "the only way out". Bitcoin and gold are weak now only because tight liquidity forces selling of winners. He expects both to surge once the market sees printing, with Bitcoin outperforming. He gives no firm timing and admits he has historically been too early.

## Main arguments

**AI as bubble (pattern recognition)**
- Each bubble (2000 tech, 2008 housing, 2020 COVID) is "built on cheap money, broken by more expensive money", then answered with Fed printing, each time at a higher level.
- AI will change the world like railroads, telegraph and internet, but overbuilding forces a sorting-out. His analogies: pets.com failed while Google and Facebook came later; WorldCom and others overbuilt fiber on "demand doubles every three months" projections.
- AI is unusually capital-intensive. He cites close to $1T of worldwide AI capex budgeted over the next few years, roughly 3-4% of US GDP, a bigger bet than railroads or the internet. He says this capital demand pushed up interest rates, and CDS on AI debt has started to blow out.
- CoreWeave: he cites about $25B of debt "the same amount for the next few years" against $10-15B revenue and thin profit. He says it traded below its IPO price and its CDS blew out. He calls it "the pin" that may pop the bubble.
- Constraints: data centers are limited by electricity, copper, components and chips. He says the "blue wave" in the US election reflects voter backlash against data centers raising power bills, which makes buildout harder.
- Concentration: a chart from his partner David Foley shows about 70% of S&P earnings growth this year is AI, with Nvidia 50-60% and Micron 10-15%. If AI rolls over, he says the index and the economy follow.
- Nvidia reported blowout numbers and guided to about "100% growth", yet closed down 2.6%. He reads this as a topping sign.
- Timing analogy: the first break in 2000 was March, the S&P hit a new high in July, and broad decline started around September, so a lag of 3 to 9 months. In 2007 the Bear Stearns CDS funds failed that summer, Bear fell in March 2008, and the crisis peaked in autumn 2008. He says things move faster now.
- He says the AI names will not make new all-time highs in this monetary environment.

**Fed, inflation, fiscal dominance**
- A new Fed chair ("Wors", likely Warsh) talks about shrinking the balance sheet and credibility. Lepard says the Fed just raised rates and the market prices about a 90% chance of 25bp and 10% of 50bp at the next meeting.
- He says inflation comes from the oil supply shock and the war in Iran plus fiscal dominance, not from rates, so the Fed is using the wrong tool. He says it is "tightening into a buzzsaw".
- He sees "foreshadowing" that the Fed expects trouble:
  - Hank Paulson's "break the glass" Treasury-market comment six months ago.
  - Fed chair speeches adding "assuming normal circumstances / not in a crisis" language.
  - The chair warning in Germany that AI could be disruptive to jobs and markets.
  - Cleveland Fed's Beth Hammack saying a plan for Treasury market dysfunction (as in March 2020) is needed.
- He says the Fed was created in 1913 as a bank bailout mechanism, and that the real mandate is keeping the financial system and bond market functioning.

**Debt math and what the print looks like**
- US debt is about $40T. FY deficit about $2T, but debt grew about $3.5T and he doesn't know where the other $1.5T went (maybe TGA refill).
- Net interest was about $1.1T, about half the deficit. Average interest rate on the debt is about 3.3%.
- Refinancing: he cites $4T in the next quarter or two and $8T next year, because debt has shifted to short maturities. Higher rates raise interest cost and deficits, which he calls a "doom loop".
- His predicted mechanism is yield curve control, not negative rates. The Fed buys its own bonds with printed money and caps yields. His reference is WWII: debt/GDP about 120%, rates fixed at 3/8% short and 2.5% long, Fed balance sheet grew 10x.
- Fed balance sheet is about $6.7T against $40T of debt. Any big roll onto the Fed would expand M2, which he says drives inflation. Pegging rates would roughly halve interest costs but be "highly inflationary".
- Other expected channels: a "national security" bailout of AI companies (race with China), stimulus checks or UBI for AI job losses under a future Democratic president, a bank-style facility like the BTFP, and a "Draghi whatever-it-takes moment". He expects the deficit to go from $2T to $4T.
- Inflation waves: COVID peak about 9, now about 3 (he calls the official numbers dishonest but the trend right). He says the next wave goes above 9, and each print must be bigger than the last.
- He sees about 10 ways out (slow inflation, fast inflation, one-time reset, re-pricing gold). Past cases he cites: Israel in the early 1980s (shekel devaluation, about 100% inflation for a few years) and Ecuador in 2000 (dollar peg, quick recovery).
- Hyperinflation of the US is a "possible" outcome. He rated it low, then said "maybe I understated the case".
- His fix, from his book: four legal-tender currencies (dollar, Bitcoin, gold, silver) with no capital gains tax on the last three, the dollar defined as a fixed or floating amount of Bitcoin or gold, and the Fed closed.
- His thesis is falsified only if the government becomes fiscally responsible, which he doesn't see.

**Bitcoin and gold**
- Gold was up 65% in 2025, which he says only happened once before, in the 1970s. This produced the "debasement trade". Bitcoin peaked at about $124k in October 2025.
- 2026 has been bad for both because liquidity is tightening. He says it shows "they have to print" and compares it to the tide going out before a tsunami.
- Disagrees with Jordy Visser that everything rises equally in a print. Stocks rise nominally, but by Gresham's law savers flee to un-debasable money, so Bitcoin and gold outperform stocks. He says Bitcoin is cheaper than gold on a relative basis.
- Technicals: BTC low about $58k, bounced into the low $80ks, now about $62k. He sees a double bottom and says price is at the floor of the power-law model. S&P support is about 7,300 (closed 7,323).
- Weimar chart (by Dan Oliver of Myrmikan Capital): gold priced in marks was extremely volatile, with sharp drops each time authorities promised sound money, but gold beat the mark overall. His point is that volatility on the road to debasement is normal.
- Advice: DCA, don't sell, don't lever, examine your thesis, put attention elsewhere (family, fitness, reading), put aside 10% of income. Bitcoin can drop 50-70%, so buy more if it does. He cites a California machinist who bought collectible gold coins from 1946 to the 1990s and left about $10M.
- Return assumption: Saylor's compound-growth estimate of 30%+ ("I'm not sure it's that high, but certainly in the 20s").
