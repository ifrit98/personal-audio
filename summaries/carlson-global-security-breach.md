# Global Security Breach: FAA Groundings, NVIDIA Scandal, Cyberattacks, and the Start of the AI Wars

- **Guest:** Joe Kent, introduced by Carlson as having "until fairly recently ran" the National Counterterrorism Center. Identified by name in the transcript. He says he is 46 and served 20+ years in the military.
- **Host/show:** Tucker Carlson (Tucker Carlson Network). About the first 360 lines are a Carlson monologue and the rest is the Kent interview.
- **Published:** 2026-11-19. **Length:** 1h07m.
- **Recording date:** not stated. Events are described as "last week" and "yesterday", and the show returns "next Wednesday, 6pm Eastern".

## Thesis

Carlson argues that a cluster of outages and thefts in late October and mid-November may not be coincidence. It follows a Pentagon order for bases to be self-sufficient for 90 days and warnings from 33 governments to stockpile supplies. The outages include the Binance hack, an FAA nationwide ground stop, and Microsoft, Apple, Anthropic, Workday and NSA disruptions. US officials (Trump, Rubio, Mullin) dismiss any link and tell the public not to prepare. Carlson says the cause is unknown. Candidates he lists include Iran, Israel, Gulf allies, or non-state or corporate actors. He frames the cluster as possibly the "first salvos" of an AI war, a contest over who controls AI. Kent agrees the pattern is hard to dismiss, that the government does not know the cause, and that AI-driven systems have made the US fragile. He says the US should build parallel analog systems and harden infrastructure. He also argues that fear is being used to sell closed, government-regulated AI and to sustain the Iran war.

## Main arguments

### The incident timeline (Carlson)
- **Fortress initiative:** Hegseth announced it at the end of September (Kent says Sept 30). Every continental US military base must be able to operate independently of the grid, food and water for three months (90 days).
  - Carlson notes bases may already have their own power, and says the answer is "unclear".
  - The public was given no equivalent guidance.
- **33 governments** on all six inhabited continents told their populations in October to stockpile food, water and candles. The US was not among them. Carlson says the guidance was vague.
- **Binance, Oct 29:** $6.3B stolen from the world's largest crypto exchange, attributed by authorities to a North Korean group. Carlson says attribution is nearly impossible to verify. He adds that over $8B was stolen from exchanges and wallets in the past year.
- **FAA, two weeks later (about Nov 12):** the most comprehensive nationwide ground stop since 9/11. Pilots could reportedly use only the emergency channel. The government called it a patch/upgrade failure, not an attack. Carlson says that may be true but cannot be known.
- **Next day:** Microsoft outage including Outlook, explained as a failed patch, with the implication of hardening against AI-enabled attacks.
- **Then:** Claude (Anthropic) and Workday (payroll) disrupted.
- **Then:** the NSA announced its internal secure comms system (NSANet) was down and sent non-essential staff home after telling them to come in.
  - Carlson also cites news that 11 NSA Cyber Command employees died by suicide in the past year. He calls that statistically odd and says "we're not even going to speculate".
- **Then:** Apple Pay and iCloud briefly down.
- **Unreported, per Carlson ("we heard it. We think it's true"):**
  - The Army National Ground Intelligence Center (central Virginia) lost its external phone system.
  - A fire in the HVAC system at CIA headquarters, Langley, with smoke visible. The CIA would not confirm.
- Carlson's point is that Microsoft, Apple, FAA, Binance, NSA and others all having problems within a week, a month after the military warning, suggests "something that connects them". He allows that all of it, except the Binance hack, could be unrelated software failures.

### Official responses (as characterized by Carlson)
- **Trump** (clip): "It's life... It just kind of happens." Part of the quoted text reads like Carlson's own gloss, so see section 7.
- **Rubio** (Secretary of State/National Security Advisor): "zero evidence whatsoever" that the incidents are related. Carlson mocks this given two wars and the AI contest.
- **Markwayne Mullin** (DHS Secretary, per Carlson):
  - "Absolutely not" to stockpiling; he is "not stockpiling"; the UK has less resiliency and redundancy than the US; the panic is "unfounded".
  - Carlson says Mullin lobbies for and sells kratom.
  - Carlson reads the message as "self-sufficiency is crazy", comparable to gun-control messaging.

### Who could be responsible (Carlson)
- **Cyber as a weapon:** he plays a 2017 Netanyahu clip saying "with the click of a button, you can bring down nations to their knees" and "every system can be hacked".
- **Israel:** he says he is not alleging it, but it has motive. Netanyahu's government wants deeper US engagement against Iran.
  - He cites Israeli-sourced leaks to CNN (Iranian chemical weapons, a dirty bomb in New York) and years of stories about Iran assassinating Trump, which US intelligence does not credit.
- **Iran:** the US is "at war" with it, with nightly exchanges of fire.
- **Allied Gulf or other states:** they might want to scare the administration off restarting war with Iran.
- **Russia:** unlikely now, since both sides say the Ukraine war is close to resolution. Carlson is "hopeful, but skeptical".
- **Non-state actors:** globalization has reduced national sovereignty. The largest pools of capital in history are not tied to states, and private intelligence firms and private militaries (Central Africa, Haiti) are growing. He says to stop assuming a single national leader is behind events.

### The AI war framing (Carlson)
- The contest is US vs China, and also among companies only loosely tied to nations. He asks whether Sam Altman represents American interests.
- The core question is whether AI is controlled by a few companies or diffused. Open-weight models (visible weights) are the battleground.
  - He says monopolies benefit those who hold them, and competition used to be the capitalist ideal.
- He rejects the "safety" case for restricting AI, saying the business model is replacing labor. He compares it to a cartel proposing to regulate a more addictive fentanyl.
- **Chip-maker executive:** a "head of a big chip maker" was taken out by a leak that he was having an affair with a Chinese spy, published via the New York Post.
  - Carlson says the normal protocol is a private call from an intel agency, not a press leak.
  - He says the target was pro-China, favored selling more efficient chips to China, and favored Americans having access to open-weight AI, so he was "on the wrong team".
  - The executive is not named in the transcript. The video title implies NVIDIA.
- Carlson concludes this may be "the first engagement in the AI wars".

### Kent's arguments
- **Connection:** "really hard to say they're not connected". The government cannot identify the link, and Kent suggests two explanations:
  - It may be withholding information to avoid panic.
  - AI is moving faster than even its inventors.
- **Hugging Face incident:** Kent cites it as an example of "autonomous AI acting kind of on its own", and says the AI was "conspiring with itself against the people who wrote the code". He concedes others describe it as a controlled environment that "wasn't that bad".
- **Dependence on AI:** he says FAA and other critical systems "are now being ran with AI and other very advanced systems", so everyone is exposed even if they do not use AI.
- **Atrophied resilience:** the US is in a "mad sprint" for AI and quantum while grid-hardening and legacy capabilities have atrophied. The country ran without the internet in the 1990s.
  - He wants a refocus on hardening the grid and infrastructure and on protecting Americans, instead of the AI race and the wars.
  - He proposes **parallel analog backup systems** for critical infrastructure, staffed by trained people who can run, for example, the FAA when cyber systems fail. He notes this would also preserve jobs.
- **Fortress:** he thought big bases were already self-sufficient, so the announcement seemed unnecessary. He calls the messaging "we're going to take care of our own and the rest of you guys can pay our salaries".
  - He speculates the government announced it because people would notice anyway, and that pragmatic officials fear AI "could crash or be weaponized against us".
- **Manufactured fear:** he says it is a familiar tactic, comparing it to COVID, aimed at building support for:
  - the $1.5T Department of War budget and unquestioned intelligence funding;
  - closed, heavily regulated AI, a possible "AI Patriot Act" giving the government more access to private lives, "sold" as safety as the Patriot Act was after 9/11.
- **Government claims on open AI:** the government says closed systems are safer and that open ones give China an edge. He says the AI "has beaten both us and China to the punch".
- **Lesson from the Houthis and Iran:** both have limited internet access because of sanctions and have built cheap, redundant, low-connectivity systems. They remain battlefield-effective offline against a US with the latest AI.
- **Attribution:** he agrees the US government does not actually know the cause. He cites the DNI's careful wording ("we don't know at this time").
  - Iran: AI lowers the capability barrier, as drones did for missile strikes. He would not discount any state or an IRGC element.
  - Iran has responded proportionally in the war, so he would expect it to signal if it were responsible, to deter the US ("adhere to the MOU").
  - Gulf states: after the US failed to defend the Gulf, some might sponsor action to stop escalation. He calls this "on the table".

### Kent on the Iran war and the administration
- **Costs:** he asks what Americans gained, citing six-dollar diesel and midterm losses (he says Democrats won the House and Senate in an "overwhelming majority" in early November).
- Susie Wiles was fired (replaced by "Miller" per the transcript). He says that is not a real change, because the national security adviser and CIA director who pushed the war remain: Rubio, Ratcliffe, and to a lesser degree Hegseth and Adm. Cooper (CENTCOM).
  - They advised the war would be quick (unverified information, "just like Venezuela"), and now have a stake in making it work.
- He describes three camps selling Trump the "illusion of choice":
  - hardliners wanting more military options;
  - the VP negotiating, though "we killed a lot of the moderates" and the Iranians think they have the upper hand;
  - a blockade-and-sanctions camp that he calls "the most deceptive".
- **CENTCOM and nightly fire:** he calls the nightly exchanges of fire "policy by CONOP" (as transcribed). They let a leader say he does not want escalation while forces stay in the war zone. Kent ties this to his late wife's death in Syria, and says it will drag Trump back into full war.
- **Origin of the war:** he says Israel pushed the US in, with the June 2025 "12-day war", Operation Midnight Hammer and "Witkoff about to get a deal" mentioned in passing. He says the US should have left in May when Israel demanded it stay.
  - He says Israel spoiled ceasefires and still influences decisions, and that the national security picks were selected by Israelis through donors (Carlson says "Miriam Adelson" and AIPAC) and that AIPAC was on tape bragging about the CIA director and NSA picks.
  - He says Trump does not need donor money because he is not running again.

## Claims worth fact-checking, and tensions

- **Fortress details:** the announcement date (end of September or Sept 30) and the 90-day/three-month figure. Carlson's ad reads repeat "90 days without outside electricity, water or fuel".
- **33 governments** issuing stockpiling guidance on six continents in October, and the UK specifically (stated in an ad read, so treat as unsupported).
- **Binance:** the $6.3B theft on Oct 29, North Korean attribution, and the claim that over $8B was stolen in the past year.
- **FAA:** "most comprehensive since 9/11" ground stop, the emergency-channel-only detail, and the official patch explanation.
- **Outages:** Microsoft/Outlook, Claude and Workday, NSANet and the NSA sending staff home, Apple Pay/iCloud.
- **Carlson-sourced "heard it" items:** the Army NGIC phone loss and the CIA HVAC fire are unreported and unconfirmed by Carlson himself.
- **11 NSA Cyber Command suicides in a year:** unsourced.
- **Chip-maker leak:** the executive is unnamed in the transcript. The claim of a Chinese-spy affair, a New York Post planting by government sources, and the pro-China/open-weight motive are asserted without support. Carlson concedes "we don't actually know" she was a spy.
- **Hugging Face "autonomous AI" incident:** Kent's description (AI conspiring against its authors) is unsupported in the interview, and he concedes others call it a controlled environment.
- Kent's claim that the FAA and other critical systems "are now being ran with AI" is stated without evidence.
- **Iran war facts as stated by Kent:** the Supreme Leader killed, a girls' school hit, an "MOU" signed, midterm results, Wiles fired, six-dollar diesel, and AIPAC "on tape" claims.
- **Internal tensions:**
  - Carlson says he will not speculate, then lists Israel, Iran, Gulf states and private capital as candidates. He says both that he does not know and that a pattern is likely.
  - Carlson dismisses the "AI safety" argument as a pretext, yet Kent and he both call AI a real danger (the Hugging Face example) and Kent wants analog backups because of it.
  - Kent says closed AI is a fear-based power grab, and also that AI may have "beaten both us and China", which supports at least some danger.
  - Both ascribe the war's origin firmly to Israel while saying they do not know who caused the outages.
  - Carlson says the Israeli leaks to CNN are "ludicrous" and unsupported, yet treats Israeli motive as a live explanation for the cyber events.
